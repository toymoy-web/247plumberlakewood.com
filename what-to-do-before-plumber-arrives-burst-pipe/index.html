What To Do Before The Plumber Arrives In Lakewood, CA

By the Lakewood 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-08

Emergency Guide

The minutes right after a pipe bursts matter more than almost any other part of the process. What you do, or don't do, in that window has more influence on the final scope of damage than anything that happens after a technician arrives. This isn't about becoming your own plumber, it's about the handful of simple steps that actually make a measurable difference before help gets there.

Step One: Shut Off The Water

If you can safely reach the main water shutoff valve, use it. In most Lakewood homes built in the 1950-53 era, this valve is located near where the water line enters the house, often in a front yard box, a garage, or near the water heater. If you don't know where yours is, this is worth locating before an emergency happens, not during one.

If the burst is isolated to a single fixture, like under a sink, and that fixture has its own shutoff valve, using that instead avoids cutting water to the whole house while you wait.

Step Two: Address Electrical Safety

Before Doing Anything Else Near The Water

  • Do not touch outlets, switches, or plugged-in appliances in or near standing water
  • If it's safe to reach the breaker panel without stepping through water, shut off power to the affected area
  • If reaching the panel means walking through water, leave it and wait for a professional instead
  • Keep children and pets away from the affected area entirely

Step Three: Limit The Spread

Once the water source is off and the area is electrically safe, move what you reasonably can, furniture, electronics, rugs, boxes, away from the wet area. Towels and a wet-vac, if you have one, can help manage water that's already pooled, but don't spend so much time on cleanup that you delay calling for help. The plumbing fix and any water damage response both benefit from starting sooner rather than later.

Step Four: Document Before You Clean

Photograph the damage before extensive cleanup begins, from multiple angles, including anything that got wet. This matters more than people expect if you end up filing a homeowner's insurance claim, since a sudden pipe burst is typically the kind of damage standard policies are built to cover, and clear documentation of the timeline and cause helps that process go smoothly.

What Not To Do

Avoid the temptation to patch the pipe yourself with tape or a clamp as anything more than a truly temporary measure, and don't run any appliance connected to the affected line until a technician confirms it's safe. Also avoid turning the water back on to "test" whether the leak stopped, since this risks worsening the damage if the pipe hasn't actually been repaired.

When It's Genuinely An Emergency Versus Urgent But Not Critical

An actively spraying or gushing pipe, water near electrical, or water spreading toward a lower floor or shared wall are all situations that warrant calling immediately, day or night. A pipe that's cracked and dripping slowly, with the main shut off and no active spread, is still worth prompt attention but may not require the same middle-of-the-night urgency.

After The Immediate Crisis

Once the water is off and a technician is on the way, the adrenaline of the moment tends to fade and it's normal to start second-guessing whether you did the right things. In most cases, shutting off the water and staying safe around electrical hazards covers the two decisions that actually matter most, everything else is secondary to those two steps.
