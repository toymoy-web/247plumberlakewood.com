Hawaiian Gardens Grew Fast After A Late Start

Hawaiian Gardens borders Lakewood to the southeast, and it's an unusual neighbor in one specific way: at just under one square mile, it's the smallest incorporated city in all of Los Angeles County. The name traces back to a 1920s refreshment stand decorated with palm fronds and bamboo at the corner of Carson Street and Norwalk Boulevard, a landmark distinctive enough that it eventually gave the whole town its name.

Before incorporating in 1964, the area was unincorporated rural land, mostly dairy and truck farming, administered under neighboring Artesia. Roads stayed unpaved dirt through the 1950s, with paving only beginning in earnest after the city incorporated. That means Hawaiian Gardens's residential construction is concentrated in a tighter, later window than Lakewood's, mostly 1960s tract housing built once the city had its own infrastructure.

The city's small footprint works in our favor for response time: there's very little of Hawaiian Gardens that's more than a few minutes from any other part of it. It's also home to The Gardens Casino, one of a small number of cities in LA County where casino gambling is legal, which generates a meaningful share of the city's revenue.

Because the entire city incorporated and built out within a fairly narrow window in the 1960s, Hawaiian Gardens doesn't have the same mixed-era complexity some of our other service areas do. We still confirm the specific property's pipe material on the first visit, but the range of what we're likely to find is narrower here than in a city like Bellflower or Downey.
