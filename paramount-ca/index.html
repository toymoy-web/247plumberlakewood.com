From Dairy Land To Housing

Paramount's Construction Spans Decades, Not One Boom

Paramount has an unusual history for this part of Los Angeles County. Through the first half of the 20th century, the area, then called Hynes and Clearwater, was the center of the region's dairy industry, home to more cows per square mile than almost anywhere west of Chicago. The two towns merged into Paramount in 1948 and incorporated as a city in 1957, and homes were built gradually as dairy land converted to residential lots, the last dairy didn't close until 1977.

That gradual, decades-long conversion means Paramount's housing stock isn't a single uniform era the way Lakewood's is. Some sections date to the immediate postwar years, others to the 1960s and 70s as the remaining dairies wound down. Our water heater repair team treats each Paramount call on its own merits rather than assuming a single citywide plumbing age.

The Paramount Hay Tree, a mature camphor tree that once served as the daily meeting point for the region's hay traders, still stands today as a California Historical Landmark, a reminder of how recently this stretch of the county was agricultural rather than residential. That transition happened within living memory for some longtime residents, which is part of why plumbing age here varies more than the city's modest size might suggest.

In practical terms, that means a Paramount homeowner shouldn't assume their neighbor's plumbing age matches their own just because the houses look similar from the street. We check each property's actual pipe material on the first visit rather than working from a citywide assumption.
