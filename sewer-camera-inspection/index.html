A sewer camera inspection is a standalone diagnostic service, separate from any repair or cleaning, for homeowners who want to know the actual condition of their line before deciding on next steps. Findings are assessed using classification criteria consistent with NASSCO's Pipeline Assessment and Certification Program (PACP), the industry-standard framework for describing pipe defects. It's the same equipment we use as part of sewer line repair and hydro jetting jobs, but you can request it on its own, without committing to a repair, if you just want information.

This comes up most often in three situations in Lakewood. That includes a recurring clog that's been snaked more than once, a pre-purchase inspection for a home built in the city's original 1950-53 construction window, and simple curiosity about a line that's never been checked in seventy years of use.

The inspection itself is non-invasive. Nothing is cut, drilled, or removed to perform it, the camera simply travels through the existing pipe via an access point that's usually already there. That makes it a low-risk way to get real information before committing to any larger decision about the line.
