Root Intrusion & Mature Trees In Lakewood, CA Drains

By the Lakewood 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-18

Homeowner Guide

Mature landscaping is one of the genuine trade-offs of an established neighborhood. Decades-old trees provide shade and character that a newer development simply doesn't have yet. Those same root systems are one of the most common reasons a drain line clogs in the same spot over and over, especially in cities like Lakewood and neighboring Torrance, where much of the tree canopy has had seventy or more years to mature.

How Roots Actually Get Into A Pipe

Tree roots seek out moisture, and a drain or sewer line carrying wastewater is an attractive, consistent source. Roots don't typically break a pipe outright to get in, they exploit small existing openings, a joint that's shifted slightly, a hairline crack, or a connection that was never perfectly sealed. Once inside, even a small root can grow rapidly in that nutrient-rich, moist environment.

Why A Snake Alone Doesn't Solve It

A cable snake cuts through or pushes past the immediate obstruction, restoring flow. What it doesn't do is remove the root mass growing inside the pipe. That's why a line that was "fixed" a few weeks ago clogs again: the root wasn't removed, just cleared aside temporarily, and it keeps growing back into the same space.

The Pattern That Confirms Root Intrusion

Same Location, Every Time

A clog that recurs in the same general area of the line, rather than different fixtures each time, points to a fixed obstruction like roots.

Shrinking Time Between Clogs

If the interval between clogs keeps getting shorter, that's consistent with a root mass continuing to grow inside the line.

Slow Drainage That Precedes A Full Clog

Roots partially restricting flow often cause gradually slower drainage in the days or weeks before a complete blockage.

What Actually Clears Root Intrusion

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full interior diameter of the pipe, which removes root mass rather than just pushing past it. This is why jetting tends to provide a longer-lasting result than repeated snaking for a root-affected line. A camera inspection before jetting confirms the cause and the location, since not every recurring clog is actually root-related.

When Jetting Isn't Enough

If root intrusion has been ongoing long enough to significantly crack or displace a section of pipe, rather than simply entering through an existing small opening, jetting clears the roots but doesn't fix the underlying structural damage. In that case, sewer line repair, whether a spot repair or trenchless lining, addresses the actual defect the roots were exploiting in the first place.

Is Removing The Tree The Answer

Removing a mature tree is a significant decision with its own trade-offs, and it doesn't always solve the problem on its own, since root systems can persist and continue growing for a period after a tree is removed. It's rarely the first recommendation, addressing the pipe itself, through jetting on a maintenance schedule or a targeted repair, is usually the more proportionate response to a recurring drain issue.

Living With Mature Trees And Older Pipes

Plenty of homes in Lakewood and Torrance manage this trade-off successfully for decades, keeping the landscaping they value while staying ahead of root intrusion through periodic jetting or camera checks rather than waiting for a full backup. The goal isn't to choose between healthy trees and healthy pipes, it's to manage both with realistic expectations about what an older drain line under mature landscaping actually needs.

A Realistic Maintenance Rhythm

For a line with a confirmed history of root intrusion, an annual or biannual jetting pass is a reasonable way to stay ahead of a full backup rather than reacting to one after it happens. Timing it before the season when root growth tends to be most active gets the most value from each visit. This is a modest, predictable cost compared to an emergency call during an actual clog.
