Faucet and valve problems are one of the highest-volume calls we get in Lakewood, and original 1950s-era fixtures are a big part of why. Many homes here are still running the same basic faucet hardware installed when the house was built, and seventy years of mineral buildup, worn washers, and corroded valve stems eventually catch up with even well-maintained fixtures.

We repair drips, replace worn cartridges and valves, and install full faucet replacements when a fixture is past the point of being worth repairing. If a leak traces back further than the faucet itself, to the supply line behind the wall, that becomes a leak detection question instead, and we'll tell you which one you're actually dealing with before quoting anything.

Kitchen sinks in particular tend to see the most wear, since they're used more often and exposed to more mineral-heavy hot water cycling than a typical bathroom fixture. If a kitchen faucet's cartridge fails, it's often worth checking the garbage disposal connection at the same time, since both share the same under-sink plumbing and a problem with one occasionally masks an issue with the other.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need This Service

Persistent Dripping

Usually a worn washer, O-ring, or cartridge, an inexpensive fix in most cases.

Low Water Pressure At One Fixture

Often mineral buildup inside the faucet aerator or valve rather than a whole-house pressure problem.

Faucet Won't Fully Shut Off

A worn valve seat or cartridge, common in original hardware this age.

Leak At The Base Or Handle

Usually a worn seal, though worth checking it isn't actually the supply line behind the wall.
