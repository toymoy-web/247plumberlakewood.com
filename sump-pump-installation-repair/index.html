A sump pump's whole job is to activate exactly when you need it most, which means a failure often isn't noticed until water is already accumulating. We repair pumps that have stopped running, install new systems for homes without one, and can add battery backup protection for properties concerned about pump failure during a power outage.

If a sump pump failure has already led to standing water, that becomes a leak detection and cleanup question as much as a pump question, and we'll help you sort out which parts of the situation need which fix.

Most Lakewood properties don't have basements, but sump pumps still show up in crawlspaces, certain low-lying yard drainage systems, and homes with a below-grade area prone to water accumulation during heavier rain. If you're not sure whether your property even has a sump pump system, that's a reasonable thing to check during any service visit.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need This Service

Pump Not Turning On

Could be a float switch issue, a power problem, or a failed motor.

Pump Running Constantly

Can indicate a stuck float switch or an undersized pump for the actual water volume.

Unusual Noise From The Pump

Grinding or rattling often signals a failing motor or debris in the impeller.

Water Around The Sump Pit Despite A Running Pump

May mean the pump is undersized or the discharge line has an issue.

If a sump pump has ever failed silently, running fine one day and not activating the next time it was needed, that's a strong argument for either a battery backup system or a water-level alarm that alerts you before an actual flood happens. A pump that "seems fine" between storms is exactly the kind of failure that goes unnoticed until it matters most.
