A backflow prevention device stops contaminated water from being siphoned back into the clean water supply, and many California water utilities require an annual test to confirm the device is still functioning correctly. If your property has an irrigation system, a fire sprinkler system, or certain commercial fixtures, there's a reasonable chance you have one of these devices and an annual testing requirement attached to it.

We test existing devices, file the required certification with the water utility, and install new backflow preventers where one is needed but not yet present. If a test reveals the device has failed, we can typically repair or replace it the same visit rather than requiring a second trip.

Requirements vary by property type and what the device protects. A residential irrigation system typically needs a simpler test than a commercial fire sprinkler connection, and some water utilities require testing annually while others work on a different cycle. We confirm your property's specific requirement rather than assuming a one-size-fits-all schedule.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need This Service

Annual Testing Notice From Your Water Utility

The most common reason for a call, a reminder that certification is due.

Visible Water Discoloration Near An Irrigation Connection

Can indicate a backflow issue worth checking even outside the annual cycle.

New Irrigation Or Fire Sprinkler Installation

New systems typically require a backflow preventer to be installed and certified before use.

Unknown Whether Your Property Has A Device

Common for newer homeowners; we can check during any service call.

If you're not sure whether your property has a backflow prevention device or when it was last tested, that's a common situation, not an unusual one, especially for a home that changed ownership without the paperwork being passed along. We can check for a device and its testing history as part of any visit.

Signs Your Backflow Device May Need Attention

  • Discolored or unusual-tasting tap water, especially after irrigation system use
  • Visible leaking or dripping from the device itself
  • A notice from your water utility that testing is overdue
  • Any recent change to your irrigation or fire sprinkler system
