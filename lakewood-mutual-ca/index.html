The Second Phase Of The 1950s Build

Plumbing Help In Lakewood Mutual

Lakewood Mutual is the tract residents simply call Mutual. It was the second phase of the postwar build, started in late 1951 as Lakewood Mutual Homes. The 1951 mutual home plan required a down payment of $695 for a two-bedroom and $795 for a three-bedroom, which put ownership within reach of many young families.

Homes from that first wave are now about 75 years old. Listing sites describe Mutual as a quiet area of ranch-style houses near Bolivar Park, between the Lakewood Country Club area and Lakewood Center. The tract has no official boundary, so we rely on your street address.

At this age, the useful plumbing question is less about a single repair and more about the long view. A house can be kept going with targeted fixes for years, and a plumber should say honestly when the repairs start to add up.

Facts About The Mutual Tract

Started In 1951

City history dates Lakewood Mutual Homes to late 1951, after the first Lakewood Park phase.

Part Of 17,500 Homes

Lakewood Park, Lakewood Mutual and Carson Park together produced about 17,500 houses by 1953.

Bolivar Park Nearby

Bolivar Park was one of three parks dedicated on the same day in September 1957.

Informal Boundaries

The name is used by residents and local sources without a formal map line.
