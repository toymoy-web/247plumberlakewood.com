One Of Lakewood's Earlier Neighborhoods

Plumbing Help In Mayfair

Mayfair is one of the older residential sections of Lakewood, and it came before the 1950 Lakewood Park tract that gave the city its postwar reputation. City history credits developer Charles Hopper with the tract, running from Ashworth Street to South Street, and notes that locals once called it Radio Park.

Mayfair Park, dedicated in 1951, was the city's only major park until 1957. The old activity building was replaced in 1990 by the John Sanford Todd Community Center and Swim Pavilion, and the park remains the neighborhood's best-known landmark. Mayfair High School shares the name.

Because Mayfair grew in stages rather than in the single three-year build of the central tracts, homes from different years can sit on neighboring blocks. That is the main reason we ask about the house before assuming anything about its pipes.

About The Area

What We Know About Mayfair

Built Before Lakewood Park

County and city histories place Mayfair among the neighborhoods that emerged from the 1930s to the late 1940s, ahead of the 1950 tract boom.

Anchored By Mayfair Park

The park opened in 1951 and still serves as the reference point residents use when they describe where they live.

Boundaries Are Informal

City history describes the original tract from Ashworth Street to South Street, though residents today may use the name more loosely.

Mixed Home Ages

Staged building means one street can differ from the next, so we check the actual pipe material on site.

Practical Guidance

Simple Checks Before You Call

A few steps help in almost any older home when a pipe fails. They are general safety steps, and the right next move depends on what you find.

Helpful Steps

  • Find the main water shutoff valve before you need it, and make sure it turns
  • Turn off electricity to any area where water is pooling near outlets
  • Move rugs, electronics and stored boxes away from wet floors
  • Take photos of the damage before cleanup in case you file an insurance claim
  • Call the plumber's line rather than waiting to see if a drip stops
