Gas line work covers everything from a suspected leak to installing a new line for an appliance like a range or a tankless water heater. In Lakewood's older homes, original gas lines and fittings from the 1950s sometimes need updating to safely support current appliances, and any suspected leak is treated as the emergency it is.

If you smell gas, specifically a rotten-egg odor added intentionally to make leaks detectable, leave the house immediately and call from outside, then call your gas utility's emergency line before calling us. Once the area is confirmed safe, our licensed technicians can locate and repair the actual source. For non-emergency work, like adding a line for a new gas water heater or an outdoor appliance, we handle installation and permitting-related documentation.

Many homes in Lakewood's original 1950s construction still have gas fittings and shutoff valves from that era, and appliance requirements have changed considerably since then. Even without an active leak, an older fitting is worth having assessed before connecting a new high-demand appliance, since matching an outdated connection to modern equipment isn't always straightforward. All work follows NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, and California's adopted amendments to it.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need This Service

Rotten-Egg Smell

The added odorant in natural gas, meant to make even a small leak noticeable. Leave the area and call from outside.

Hissing Sound Near A Gas Appliance Or Line

Worth immediate attention even without an obvious odor.

Dead Or Dying Vegetation Near An Underground Line

Can indicate an underground gas leak affecting soil conditions.

Pilot Light That Won't Stay Lit

Not always a leak, but worth having checked, since it can also indicate a supply issue.
