A burst pipe is one of the few plumbing problems that genuinely can't wait, and it's a call we treat as an emergency every time, day or night. In Lakewood, original galvanized and copper supply lines from the 1950-53 construction window are now well past a normal service life, and a sudden crack or full break in an aging line is one of the more common causes we see. A burst can also happen at any age from a hard freeze, physical damage, or a manufacturing defect.

The first priority on any burst pipe call is stopping the water, then diagnosing and repairing the actual break. If the burst turns out to be connected to a broader issue, such as widespread corrosion throughout the home's original water heater connections or supply lines, we'll walk you through repair versus repiping options honestly.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need This Service

Water Spraying Or Pooling Suddenly

The clearest sign of an active burst, shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely.

Sudden Drop In Water Pressure

Can indicate a break somewhere in the line even without visible water yet.

Water Stains Spreading On Ceiling Or Wall

A burst inside a wall or ceiling cavity often shows up this way before it's visibly spraying.

Unusually High Water Meter Reading

Worth checking immediately if you suspect a hidden burst in a section not directly visible.

While You Wait For A Technician

  • Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely
  • Turn off electricity to any area where water is pooling near outlets or fixtures
  • Move furniture, electronics, and valuables away from the affected area
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup starts, useful for an insurance claim
  • Avoid using appliances connected to the affected line until a technician has checked it

Temporary Fix vs. Professional Repair

Temporary Measure

  • Pipe tape or a clamp after the water is shut off
  • Buys time only, doesn't actually fix the break
  • Risk of the leak worsening if pressure is restored
  • Not a substitute for an actual repair

Professional Repair

  • Addresses the actual break in the line
  • Matches pipe material correctly
  • Pressure-tested before the job is called done
  • The only way to safely restore full water service
