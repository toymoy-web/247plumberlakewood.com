Repiping is a bigger decision than a single repair, and it's one we only recommend when the numbers actually support it. Most of Lakewood was built between 1950 and 1953, and a lot of homes here are on their original galvanized supply lines, now well past seventy years old. Reaching a point where repeated leaks in different spots start costing more over time than a full replacement would is a real, common milestone for a home this age.

We walk through the real comparison with you: how many repairs you've already had, what a repipe would cost, and whether your specific situation justifies it yet. All work follows California's adopted Uniform Plumbing Code. If you've had one or two isolated leaks rather than a pattern, leak detection and a targeted repair is usually the better value, and we'll say so rather than push a bigger job than you need.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need This Service

Repeated Leaks In Different Locations

The clearest sign that the pipe material itself, not a single fitting, is failing.

Persistently Low Water Pressure Throughout The House

Internal corrosion narrowing multiple sections of galvanized pipe, not just one fixture's supply line.

Discolored Water From Multiple Fixtures

Rust and mineral buildup affecting the whole system rather than one isolated pipe.

Visible Corrosion On Exposed Pipe Sections

A visual warning sign worth having assessed even before a leak actually occurs.

If you've had more than two unrelated pipe leaks in the past year or two, that pattern by itself is worth a repiping conversation, even if each individual leak seemed minor. Original galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, so visible failures in different spots usually mean the whole system is reaching the same point at once, not that you've just had bad luck.

Copper vs. PEX

Which Fits Your Home

Copper

  • Longer track record over many decades
  • Resists most chemical corrosion well
  • More labor-intensive to install
  • Higher material cost than PEX

PEX

  • Faster installation, less demolition
  • Flexible, fewer joints needed
  • Lower material and labor cost
  • Shorter track record than copper
