Lakewood's water runs moderately hard, and hard water is a big part of why water heaters and fixtures here sometimes need service before their rated lifespan would suggest. Mineral buildup from hard water accelerates sediment accumulation inside a tank, narrows fixture aerators over time, and leaves the white mineral crusting many homeowners notice around faucets and showerheads.

We install and repair both water softeners and whole-home filtration systems, and we check the condition of the existing system before recommending a replacement. Installations follow NSF/ANSI 44 standards for water softener performance and California's adopted Uniform Plumbing Code for the connection itself. If a system's real problem turns out to be a valve or control head issue rather than the unit itself, a repair is usually the more cost-effective fix, and we'll say so rather than push a new install by default. If your water heater has already shown signs of heavy sediment, addressing water hardness at the source can slow how quickly that problem returns.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need This Service

White Mineral Buildup

Crusty white deposits around faucets, showerheads, and fixture aerators.

Reduced Soap Lather

Hard water makes soap and shampoo harder to lather and rinse fully.

Spotty Dishes And Glassware

Mineral spots left behind even after a full dishwasher cycle.

Water Heater Needing Service More Often

Hard water accelerates sediment buildup, a common contributor to earlier-than-expected water heater problems.

If an existing softener or filtration system isn't performing the way it used to, that's worth a closer look rather than an assumption. Running constantly, using far more salt than before, or simply not producing the soft-water result it once did are all signs the control valve or resin bed needs attention. That's often a sign of a fixable problem, not a sign the whole unit is failing.

Softener vs. Filtration

Which One Do You Need

Water Softener

  • Removes calcium and magnesium hardness
  • Reduces scale buildup in pipes and fixtures
  • Uses salt for regeneration
  • Doesn't remove chlorine taste or odor

Whole-Home Filtration

  • Improves taste and removes chlorine/odor
  • Can target sediment and other contaminants
  • No salt or regeneration cycle
  • Doesn't address hardness on its own
