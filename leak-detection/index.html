Leak detection is one of our highest-volume calls, and most of them start the same way: a water bill that jumped for no obvious reason, or a damp spot with no visible source. Most homes share the same 1950-53 plumbing age, whether in Lakewood Mutual's mid-century tracts or the larger lots near Lakewood Park. A hidden leak in a supply line, behind a wall, or under a slab is common enough here that guessing at the source usually wastes more money than it saves.

We use electronic leak detection equipment to pinpoint the source before opening any wall or concrete, rather than cutting exploratory holes and hoping. If tracing points to a leak directly under the slab, that becomes a slab leak repair instead of a standard line fix, and we'll tell you which one you're actually dealing with before quoting anything.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need This Service

Water Bill Spike With No Explanation

If usage habits haven't changed but the bill jumped, a running leak is the most common cause.

Warm Spot Or Damp Patch On The Floor

Often points to a hot water line leaking under a slab, worth checking before it spreads.

Sound Of Running Water With Everything Off

If you can hear water moving with every fixture shut off, there's a leak somewhere in the line.

Meter Still Running With The House Shut Off

Confirms a leak between the meter and the house rather than an individual fixture.

If the water meter keeps turning even with every fixture in the house shut off, that alone confirms a leak somewhere between the meter and the house, not a fixture problem. The same goes for a water bill that climbed with no change in how the house is used. Both are worth a detection visit before assuming where the leak is.
