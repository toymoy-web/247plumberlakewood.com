A City Built By The Aerospace Boom

Downey's Population Quadrupled In Twenty Years

Downey's housing stock traces almost directly to one industry: aerospace. When North American Aviation expanded its Downey plant after World War II, and later built the Apollo spacecraft there, the city's population surged from around 12,500 in 1940 to over 82,000 by 1960. That kind of sustained growth over two full decades, rather than a single short building boom, means Downey's housing spans a wider range of construction years than a city like Lakewood that went up in a single three-year window.

Homes from the earliest part of that boom, the late 1940s, are now pushing eight decades old, while houses built as the boom continued into the late 1950s and early 1960s are somewhat younger. Either way, original supply lines and drain systems from any point in that stretch are well past the age where problems become common. Our leak detection team treats Downey calls with that wider age range in mind rather than assuming one fixed plumbing profile, and every repair follows California's adopted Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) regardless of a home's construction era.

Rancho Los Amigos, now a national rehabilitation hospital dating back to 1888, predates the aerospace boom entirely and sits as a reminder that Downey's history runs deeper than its postwar identity suggests. For plumbing purposes, though, it's the 1946-to-mid-1960s residential expansion that matters most, since that's when the vast majority of the city's current housing stock actually went in the ground.

With roughly 60 percent of the city's dwellings being single-family homes, a larger share than some denser neighbors, most Downey plumbing calls involve a standalone property rather than a shared or multi-unit line, which typically makes diagnosis and repair more straightforward.
